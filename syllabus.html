MHE 301 develops the quantitative design of materials handling equipment, from continuous bulk conveying through discrete unit-load handling to storage system layout.

Course Meeting Times

Lectures2 sessions per week, 1.5 hours each (3 hours total)
Recitation1 hour per week, optional problem session
Self-studyApproximately 6 hours per week
Duration15 teaching weeks plus examination period

Prerequisites

  • Engineering Mechanics — statics and dynamics of rigid bodies
  • Mechanics of Materials — stress, strain, and factor of safety
  • Machine Design or equivalent — shafts, bearings, belts, gearing, motor selection
  • Recommended: an introductory course in fluid mechanics

Description

Materials handling is the movement, storage, protection and control of materials throughout manufacture, distribution, consumption and disposal. It typically consumes 20–50 % of total operating cost in a plant while adding no value to the product. Good handling engineering therefore targets the lowest total cost per tonne moved, at an acceptable level of risk to people and product.

The course treats each equipment class in the same way: understand the material, establish the required throughput, size the machine, compute the resistances and power, check the critical stresses, and select real components. Design calculations follow ISO 5048, DIN 22101 and CEMA practice.

Learning Outcomes

  1. Characterise bulk and unit materials and match them to a suitable class of handling equipment.
  2. Size a continuous conveyor for a required capacity, including cross-section, belt width and speed.
  3. Compute motion resistances, drive power, and belt or chain tensions using standard methods.
  4. Select belts, idlers, pulleys, drives, take-ups and safety devices from manufacturers' data.
  5. Design transfer points and evaluate layouts against safety and maintenance requirements.
  6. Compare handling alternatives on a life-cycle cost basis.

Examination policy

Both examinations are open-book for standards tables, charts and a personal formula sheet. Calculators are required; programmable devices with stored solutions are not permitted. All numerical answers must carry units and a stated number of significant figures.

Design Project

Each team receives a handling duty — a material, a required throughput, a geometry and a site constraint — and must deliver a complete design:

  1. Material characterisation and equipment selection with justification of the alternatives rejected.
  2. Full sizing calculations: capacity, speed, resistances, power, tensions.
  3. Component selection from real manufacturers' catalogues, with part numbers.
  4. General arrangement drawing and transfer-point detail.
  5. Safety review and maintenance schedule.
  6. Capital and operating cost estimate over a 10-year life.

Policies

Late work

Problem sets lose 10 % per calendar day late, to a floor of zero after five days. Extensions are granted for documented illness or emergency only.

Collaboration

Discussion of problem sets is encouraged; the submitted solution must be written independently. Design project work is collaborative within the team, and each report names the contribution of every member.

Use of software and AI tools

Spreadsheets, CAD and conveyor design software are permitted and encouraged. Any calculation performed by software must be accompanied by a hand check of at least one representative case. Where a generative AI tool is used, its use must be declared, and the student remains fully responsible for the correctness of every number submitted.

Standards

  • ISO 5048 — Continuous mechanical handling equipment — Belt conveyors with carrying idlers — Calculation of operating power and tensile forces.
  • DIN 22101 — Continuous conveyors — Belt conveyors for loose bulk materials — Basis for calculation and dimensioning.
  • ISO 22721 — Conveyor belts — Specification for rubber- or plastics-covered conveyor belts of textile construction for underground mining.
  • ISO 1537, ISO 1536 — Troughed and flat belt conveyor idlers.
