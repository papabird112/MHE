Course Description

Materials handling accounts for a large share of the total cost of manufacturing and distribution, yet it adds no value to the product itself. The engineer's task is therefore to move material with the least cost, the least damage and the least risk to people, while meeting a required throughput.

This course develops the analysis and design of the principal classes of materials handling equipment. It begins with continuous conveying of bulk solids — belt, chain, screw, bucket and pneumatic conveyors — then moves to discrete unit-load handling with cranes, hoists, industrial trucks and automated guided vehicles, and closes with storage system design and the economic justification of handling investments.

Emphasis throughout is on quantitative design: computing capacity, resistances, power, tensions and component ratings from recognised standards (ISO 5048, DIN 22101, CEMA), and then selecting real hardware against those numbers.

Learning Outcomes

On successful completion of this course a student will be able to:

  1. Characterise a bulk or unit material and select an appropriate class of handling equipment for a given duty.
  2. Compute the required capacity, cross-sectional loading and belt speed of a continuous conveyor.
  3. Determine motion resistances, drive power, belt tensions and take-up requirements using standard methods.
  4. Select belts, idlers, pulleys, drives and safety devices from manufacturers' data.
  5. Lay out a handling system, including transfer points, and evaluate it against safety and maintenance requirements.
  6. Justify a materials handling investment on a life-cycle cost basis.

Course Structure

Part I — Bulk Solids

Chapters 1–5. Continuous conveying: belt, chain, screw, bucket and pneumatic systems.

Part II — Unit Loads

Chapters 6–7. Cranes, hoists, industrial trucks, AGVs and unit-load formation.

Part III — Systems

Chapter 8. Storage, warehousing, layout and economic evaluation.

Prerequisites
